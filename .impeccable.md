## Design Context

### Users

Pomogit is for solo software developers who want a focused bridge between intention and shipped work. They plan concrete outcomes, protect time to finish them, and close sessions with evidence such as a commit, pull request, or written result.

The product should reduce planning friction and make progress inspectable. It is not a generic Pomodoro timer, social network, or productivity-score dashboard.

### Brand Personality

Graphic, direct, assured.

Pomogit should feel like a carefully designed independent software product with the directness and graphic economy of Sprout: confident typography, plainspoken copy, useful humor in small details, and no ornamental polish that obscures the product.

The primary brand mark is the supplied woven geometric Pomogit symbol. Use it without redrawing or decorating it, preserve its square proportions, and adapt its monochrome color only for light/dark contrast.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use Sprout as the sole dominant visual reference: a nearly white canvas, near-black grotesk typography, acid-lime primary actions, thin neutral rules, compact navigation, sparse pixel-like brand details, generous whitespace, and simple outlined modules.

Replace the cream and forest-heavy palette. Light mode should be overwhelmingly white and black with lime used only for actions and current selection. Dark mode should invert the neutral field while keeping lime controlled. Drop glassmorphism, decorative ASCII, atmospheric gradients, soft auras, giant framed cards, random glyphs, conventional dashboard tiles, and generic blue-purple effects.

Layouts should be flat, crisp, and grid-aware. Use small radii, one-pixel outlines, firm rectangular controls, and almost no shadows. Motion should be quick and functional, using opacity and transform only.

The product shell must always answer three questions without interpretation: where am I, what is moving, and where can I go next. Desktop uses a quiet utility sidebar inspired by compact browser sidebars: a customizable row of three shortcuts, then one authoritative vertical list for Home / Board / Canvas / Worklog. Home summarizes the system; Board owns task flow; Canvas owns spatial thinking. Focus is a temporary mode entered from a task, not a competing organizational page. Mobile uses a compact page header and labeled bottom navigation.

Keep marketing composition on the landing page. Inside the workspace, use fixed-size application typography, compact page titles, section rules, and task-dense layouts. A product route should feel like a specific working surface, never like another homepage with a giant slogan.

On desktop, the sidebar sits directly on the application background outside the rounded workspace frame. Only the page region is wrapped: its persistent header and independently scrolling body share one outlined, rounded surface. Keep the navigation rail visually quiet and borderless so it reads as the shell around the workspace, not as a second card. Its width is user-resizable from the seam, persists locally, and moves the workspace edge with it. Do not add redundant locality labels to the rail. Interactive controls throughout the product use a restrained machined treatment: cool-neutral vertical gradients, hairline borders, inset highlights, and shallow pressed states. Inputs use the same material more quietly and remain visually recessed. Lime gradients are reserved for primary actions and active selections; content surfaces stay flat. Hover feedback never moves controls or changes surrounding geometry; use finish, border, color, and inset depth in place. Keep workspace pages short enough that they do not need a second navigation layer. Search and New shortcuts move focus directly to the relevant control.

### Design Principles

1. Make Pomogit recognizable through graphic economy, not decoration.
2. Let the work outcome lead; the timer supports it.
3. Show the real product as the visual centerpiece.
4. Use white space, black type, thin rules, and rare lime accents to create hierarchy.
5. Close every completed session with inspectable evidence.
6. Preserve accessibility, reduced motion, keyboard use, and responsive composition.
7. Never rely on icon-only navigation or color alone to communicate location and progress.
8. Separate global journey switching from page and section navigation.
9. Prefer quiet utility surfaces over diagrammatic navigation or oversized containers.
10. Keep the sidebar outside one rounded page wrapper, with the persistent header inside that wrapper.

## Implementation Brief

### Feature Summary

Rebuild the local-first product as a complete, navigable prototype with six connected experiences:

1. **Landing** — an art-directed introduction that shows the real product and opens the local workspace.
2. **Home** — the default overview for current work, next work, workspace totals, and recent evidence.
3. **Board** — the full Kanban and task-creation surface for Inbox, Ready, In focus, and Done tasks.
4. **Canvas** — an open surface for movable task pins, movable notes, free drawing, and erasing.
5. **Focus** — a distraction-free mode with pause, resume, refresh recovery, and completion handling.
6. **Worklog** — a searchable record of evidence-backed outcomes.

No AWS, account, or backend is required. Browser storage remains the source of truth while the product loop is validated.

### Primary User Action

Choose an outcome, focus on it, and leave evidence that the work moved forward.

### Visual Direction

Translate Sprout's graphic system closely into an original Pomogit product: near-white ground, near-black grotesk type, acid-lime actions, thin gray rules, compact mono metadata, small-radius rectangles, and simple iconographic details. Use licensed open-source alternatives for Sprout's proprietary typography. Do not copy its logo, illustrations, source code, or proprietary assets.

### Layout Strategy

The landing page uses a compact masthead, a decisive statement, and a working overview preview as the hero object. The application uses a calm shell with Home, Board, Canvas, and Worklog navigation.

Home is a concise cross-section rather than another work surface. Board owns task organization: capture an outcome, move it between visible states, or start it immediately. Canvas shares the same task records rather than creating a second task system. Focus gives the active outcome and deadline the strongest hierarchy. Worklog uses rows and filters rather than repeated cards.

The shortcut row has three persistent user-controlled slots. Pages and tasks can be pinned through a custom context menu; New, Focus, and Canvas are the defaults. Every context action also has a visible menu-button path on task cards.

### Key States

- First visit landing page and return-to-workspace behavior.
- Empty and populated Home overview.
- Empty and populated Kanban columns, including drag-and-drop movement on the dedicated Board route.
- Empty and populated canvas, movable task pins and notes, drawing, erasing, and item removal.
- Custom context menus and persistent shortcut replacement.
- Invalid and valid focus setup.
- Running, paused, resumed, manually ended, and naturally completed session.
- Refresh recovery from an absolute deadline.
- Commit, pull-request, and written-result closure.
- Empty, populated, filtered, and no-match history.
- Light, dark, keyboard, mobile, and reduced-motion layouts.

### Interaction Model

Navigation changes local views without a backend or router dependency. Saving an outcome adds it to Inbox. Starting focus moves its shared task into the active state. Timer math derives remaining time from timestamps. Completion opens an evidence step, moves the task to Done, and adds it to Worklog. Canvas pins retain task identity across states. All changes persist immediately in browser storage.

### Content Requirements

Prefer short working language: “Home,” “Inbox,” “Ready,” “Start focus,” “Pause,” “Add evidence,” “Canvas,” and “Search worklog.” Examples should resemble real software work. Avoid productivity claims, streak language, vague inspiration, and redundant explanatory copy.

### Recommended References

- Spatial design for editorial composition and app-shell adaptation.
- Typography for marketing-to-product hierarchy.
- Color and contrast for dual green-forward themes.
- Interaction design for the queue, timer, and evidence forms.
- Motion design for entrances and focus-state feedback.
- Responsive design for landing and application modes.
- UX writing for short labels, errors, and empty states.

### Deferred

- GitHub OAuth and automatic issue or commit synchronization.
- Multi-user collaboration.
- Cloud persistence and deployment.
- Analytics that have not proved actionable through local use.
